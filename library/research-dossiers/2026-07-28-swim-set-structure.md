## 1. Executive summary

- **Warm-up: that a warm-up helps is evidence-backed; the specific "≈X% of
  session distance" proportion is convention, not evidence.** McGowan et al.
  (2015), the standard review, verifies that an active warm-up raises muscle
  temperature, speeds VO2 kinetics, and improves subsequent performance —
  including swimming-specific applications. But it does **not** license a
  numeric "warm-up should be 15–25% of the session" rule; that proportion
  (visible in the `sample_pool_workout_*` files at ~20–25%) is
  **`Coach judgment` / practitioner convention**, honestly labelled.
- **Main-set formats (straight repeats, descending sets, broken-distance,
  pyramids, negative-split segments): no verified source establishes one
  format as superior for aerobic CSS-anchored training.** These are the
  shared vocabulary of pool coaching, illustrated in the sample workouts, but
  this pass found **no experimental evidence** ranking them. They are
  `Coach judgment` — legitimate, widely used, but not evidence-graded.
  Negative-split *pacing* is the one exception with real swim evidence, and it
  is already grounded in `04` (Saavedra et al. 2018; the ~873k-runner Berlin
  analysis) — `14` should cross-reference `04` rather than re-derive it.
- **Varying structure by training phase: this one *does* have swim-specific
  evidence, but it is about intensity distribution, not set geometry.**
  González-Ravé et al. (2021), a systematic review of elite swimmers, found
  coaches shift the *training-intensity distribution* across a macrocycle —
  broadly pyramidal in the general/preparatory phase, moving toward
  threshold/polarized in the specific/competitive phase — and Pla et al.
  (2019) is an intervention trial showing polarized slightly out-performed
  threshold over 6 weeks. This **supports the *principle*** that session
  emphasis should shift base→build (more continuous aerobic volume early,
  more race-pace/broken work later). It does **not** validate any specific
  broken-distance set design — so `14` may tag the base-vs-build *direction*
  as `[EVIDENCE: swim]` (intensity-distribution evidence) while keeping the
  concrete set *shapes* as `Coach judgment`.
- **Net for `14`:** a warm-up/main-set/cool-down *skeleton* with proportions
  and format menu = `Coach judgment`/convention; negative-split pacing =
  cross-reference `04`'s existing `[EVIDENCE: swim]`; base→build shift in
  emphasis = `[EVIDENCE: swim]` (intensity distribution), Confidence medium,
  with the caveat that the evidence is elite-swimmer and about distribution,
  not this athlete's specific aerobic-session geometry.

## 2. Verified sources by topic

### Topic 1 — Warm-up efficacy (grounds *that* a warm-up belongs, not its size)

- **✓ McGowan C.J., Pyne D.B., Thompson K.G., Rattray B. (2015)** —
  "Warm-Up Strategies for Sport and Exercise: Mechanisms and Applications" —
  *Sports Medicine*, 45(11):1523–1546.
  **Verification:** title, authors, journal, year confirmed via publisher
  (Springer) and PubMed listings; the author group is the same one behind the
  elite-swimming warm-up-practice work, and swimming applications are
  discussed in the review. (Volume/pages 45(11):1523–1546 taken from standard
  citation records; the identity of the paper is not in doubt.)
  **Summary:** narrative review of warm-up mechanisms — temperature,
  metabolic, neural, and psychological effects; active warm-up raises muscle/
  core temperature, elevates baseline VO2 and speeds VO2 kinetics, and can
  potentiate subsequent performance, with attention to the transition phase
  between warm-up and event (including swimming, e.g. dryland activation and
  heat-retention during pool-deck transitions).
  **Why it matters — and its limit:** grounds the *inclusion* of a warm-up in
  a structured session as `[EVIDENCE: general-endurance/swim]`. It does **not**
  prescribe a warm-up *volume* as a share of session distance — that
  proportion is convention. Do not stretch this citation into a numeric rule.
  **Tag:** `[ADAPTED: general-endurance]` leaning swim-applicable (the review
  spans sport broadly but explicitly addresses swimming); Confidence:
  medium-high for "warm-up helps," not applicable to proportion sizing.

### Topic 2 — Periodization / phase-varying structure (base vs. build/peak)

- **✓ González-Ravé J.M., Hermosilla F., González-Mohíno F., Casado A.,
  Pyne D.B. (2021)** — "Training Intensity Distribution, Training Volume, and
  Periodization Models in Elite Swimmers: A Systematic Review" —
  *International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance*, 16(7):913–926.
  **Verification:** title, full author list, journal, year, volume/issue/
  pages confirmed via PubMed and the publisher (Human Kinetics).
  **Summary:** systematic review (9 studies: 3 interventions, 6 retrospective).
  Elite swimmers' training-intensity distribution (TID) tracks event
  specialty — sprinters polarized/threshold, middle-distance
  threshold/pyramidal, long-distance primarily **pyramidal** — and coaches
  commonly shift TID across the macrocycle (pyramidal in general/preparatory
  work → threshold/polarized toward competition), often in **wave-like
  (undulating) mesocycles** rather than strictly linear progression. Authors
  explicitly caution the evidence base is thin and it is "unclear whether a
  different periodization approach would yield better results."
  **Why it matters:** the real swim-specific support for varying session
  *emphasis* by phase — and it points at **pyramidal/aerobic-heavy for the
  long-distance base**, which matches an ultra-swim base phase well. But it is
  about **intensity distribution across the plan**, not about the internal
  geometry of a single aerobic session — do not over-read it as validating a
  specific broken-distance main set.
  **Tag:** `[EVIDENCE: swim]` for the base→build shift in emphasis;
  Confidence: medium (systematic review but thin/observational base, elite
  population, and it is distribution-level not session-geometry-level).

- **✓ Pla R., Le Meur Y., Aubry A., Toussaint J.F., Hellard P. (2019)** —
  "Effects of a 6-Week Period of Polarized or Threshold Training on
  Performance and Fatigue in Elite Swimmers" — *International Journal of
  Sports Physiology and Performance*, 14(2):183–189.
  **Verification:** title, full author list, journal, year, volume/issue/
  pages confirmed via PubMed and the publisher.
  **Summary:** intervention study in elite swimmers; a 6-week **polarized**
  block produced a small-to-moderately greater 100-m improvement (~0.97% vs.
  ~0.09%) and less perceived fatigue than a **threshold** block, with no
  meaningful difference in physiological markers.
  **Why it matters:** corroborates that *how intensity is distributed within a
  block* measurably affects outcome in swimmers — supporting the general idea
  that phase structure is not cosmetic. Caveat: 100-m performance in elite
  swimmers is far from this athlete's ultra-distance aerobic use-case; cite it
  as supporting the *principle* that block emphasis matters, not as a
  prescription for her sessions.
  **Tag:** `[EVIDENCE: swim]` for "block intensity emphasis affects outcome";
  Confidence: medium (small, elite, short, sprint-outcome — directionally
  supportive, not directly transferable).

### Topic 3 — Set-format menu and warm-up/main/cool-down proportions

**Finding: no verified experimental source establishes the proportions or
ranks the set formats.** Straight repeats, descending sets, broken-distance/
pyramid sets, and negative-split segments are the standard coaching
vocabulary (illustrated, not evidenced, in `sample_pool_workout_openwater_
focus.md` and `sample_pool_workout_traditional.md`, which show warm-ups at
roughly 20–25% of total distance, a preset/activation block, a multi-block
main set, and a short cool-down). This pass found **no citable study**
comparing these formats for aerobic CSS-anchored training or fixing a
warm-up percentage. Therefore, for `14`:

- Warm-up/main-set/cool-down **proportions** (e.g. warm-up ~15–25%, cool-down
  ~10%): `Coach judgment:` / practitioner convention. Confidence: n/a
  (convention).
- Main-set **format menu** (straight repeats / descending / broken-distance /
  pyramid): `Coach judgment:` — legitimate, standard, but not evidence-ranked.
- **Negative-split pacing** within a segment: this is the one format element
  with real swim evidence, and it already lives in `04` (Saavedra, Einarsson
  et al. 2018 `[EVIDENCE: swim]`; plus the `[ADAPTED: running]` Berlin
  analysis). `14` should **cross-reference `04`**, not re-cite or re-derive.
