## 1–3. Snowdonia Watersports — "Open Water Swimming Training: Enhance
Your Skills and Train With Purpose"

**Source:** Dan McGonigle, Snowdonia Watersports Ltd blog, July 1, 2023.
https://snowdoniawatersports.com/blogs/news/open-water-swimming-training-enhance-your-skills

### 1. Session 1 — Long Endurance Swim
> Warm-up: 10-15 minutes of easy swimming, focusing on technique.
> Main set: Swim continuously for 60-90 minutes, maintaining a steady
> pace. Aim to cover a distance slightly longer than your race goal.
> Cool-down: 5-10 minutes of easy swimming, focusing on recovery.

**Duration:** ~80-115 min total (stated as ranges); no fixed distance
given (duration-based, not distance-based).

**Classification: novel.** A single unbroken continuous swim with no rep
structure whatsoever — none of the six taxonomy shapes apply, since all
six presuppose a main set built from discrete reps. This is the single
most important structural finding of this file: real open-water "main
sets" are frequently not rep-based at all.

### 2. Session 2 — Sighting and Navigation
> Warm-up: 10-15 minutes of easy swimming, incorporating sighting drills.
> Main set: Swim for 30-45 minutes in open water, focusing on sighting
> every 10-12 strokes. Introduce variations such as swimming towards
> different landmarks or buoys.
> Cool-down: 5-10 minutes of easy swimming, focusing on recovery.

**Duration:** ~45-70 min total; no fixed distance given.

**Classification: novel** — same single-continuous-swim shape as Session
1, with an embedded technique-frequency cue (sight every 10-12 strokes)
rather than any pace/distance-rep structure.

### 3. Session 3 — 400m Repeats
> Warm-up: 10-15 minutes of easy swimming, focusing on technique.
> Main set: Perform 6-8 x 400m repeats at your target race pace. Allow
> 30-90 seconds of active recovery between each repeat.
> Cool-down: 5-10 minutes of easy swimming, focusing on recovery.

**Distance:** 6-8 × 400 m (2,400-3,200 m main set).

**Classification: straight-continuous-reps.** Uniform reps, one constant
target pace — the one session in this whole file that cleanly matches an
existing taxonomy shape, and notably the only one of the six sessions
researched here that is actually distance/rep-based rather than
duration-based.

## 4–6. TrainingPeaks — "3 Best Workouts for Open Water Swimming"

**Source:** Melissa Mantak, TrainingPeaks blog (publication date not
shown on the fetched page).
https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/workouts-for-open-water-swimming/

### 4. Workout 1 — Swim Straight/Navigation
> Warm up 5-15 minutes with swim drills and breathing rhythm work.
> Perform the swim-straight drill to assess your directional accuracy.
> Execute a prolonged steady aerobic swim focusing on sighting technique
> and directional control.

**Duration/distance:** not specified (open-ended "prolonged" swim).

**Classification: novel** — same single-continuous-technique-focus shape
as Snowdonia sessions 1-2, with a short calibration drill (swim-straight
check) bolted on the front rather than a rep-based main set.

### 5. Workout 2 — Strength/Power (Polo Drill)
> Warm up 5-15 minutes with drills and breathing work. Polo Drill:
> complete 3-10 rounds alternating 4 strokes of polo swimming with 10
> strokes of normal swimming, relaxing between sets. Best performed in
> shorter sections or distributed throughout an extended swim.

**Duration/distance:** not specified — the rep unit here is *strokes*, not
distance or time.

**Classification: novel.** A stroke-count-based alternating micro-interval
(4 strokes head-up "polo" style / 10 strokes normal) embedded inside a
continuous open-water swim. None of the six taxonomy shapes are defined in
stroke-count units — this is a genuinely different organizing variable
(strokes, not meters or seconds) from anything in the current menu.

### 6. Workout 3 — Speed Skill/Race Efforts
> Warm up 5-15 minutes with drills and breathing preparation. Polo Drill:
> 1-3 rounds of 4 polo strokes alternating with 10 normal strokes. Speed
> work: 3-10 rounds of 10 fast-arm strokes followed by 10 relaxed strokes.
> Progress to race-pace intervals or full race distance after 1-2 rounds.

**Duration/distance:** not specified except "full race distance" as a
late-session option.

**Classification: novel** — same stroke-count-alternation shape as
Workout 2, escalating into an open-ended "race-pace intervals or full race
distance" tail. Still not reducible to one of the six shapes.

## Summary tally (this file, 6 open-water workouts)

- **straight-continuous-reps:** 1 (#3 — the 400m repeats session)
- **descend / pyramid / negative-split / broken-distance-lite /
  broken-distance ladder:** 0
- **novel:** 5 (#1, #2, #4, #5, #6)

**The dominant novel shape here is structural, not just a set-geometry
variant:** 4 of 6 sessions (#1, #2, #4, and half of #6) are built around a
single **continuous, duration-bounded swim** with no internal rep
structure at all — "swim for 60-90 minutes," "swim for 30-45 minutes,"
"prolonged steady aerobic swim" — which the entire six-shape taxonomy is
silent on, since every one of those six shapes assumes a main set made of
discrete, countable reps. The other novel shape, appearing twice (#5, #6),
is a **stroke-count-based alternating interval** (e.g. "4 strokes polo / 10
strokes normal") — a rep unit measured in strokes rather than meters or
seconds, which is also outside anything the current taxonomy addresses.

**Practical implication for template design:** if this small, real sample
is at all representative, an open-water-specific extension to the format
menu needs at minimum (a) a "continuous duration swim, no reps" shape
(with optional technique-frequency cue, e.g. sighting-every-N-strokes),
and (b) a stroke-count-based alternating-interval shape — neither of which
resembles the pool-oriented, meters/seconds-based six-shape menu this file
was checked against.
