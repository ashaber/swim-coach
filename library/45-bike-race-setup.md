# Bike race setup and maintenance (brakes, wheels, sealant, drivetrain, pre-race checks)

See `00-conventions.md` for the tagging scheme and `reference_list.md` for citations. Companions:
`44-tire-pressure.md` (pressure is not repeated here), `42-ultra-mtb-endurance.md` (6-24 h events).

**Sport scope: `bike`.** **UNREVIEWED**, pending human review. Written for a masters amateur
cyclist moving from CX toward 6-24 h MTB. **Evidence is thin here:** there is essentially no
peer-reviewed research on bike maintenance. Almost everything below is manufacturer or tool-maker
guidance (commercial interest in selling pads, tools, lube) or practitioner opinion, and is
labelled so. The one independent measurement is the chain-efficiency lab test in section 5. **Out
of scope: a full bike fit**, see section 9.

## 1. Disc brake bed-in (UNREVIEWED)

Manufacturer and practitioner guidance. Bed-in transfers pad material into the rotor's pores
through braking heat (Park Tool, as quoted by Singletracks). Makers disagree on detail: SRAM about
20 slows then 10 harder; TRP 15-20 then 10-15; Hayes 50 at or below 15 mph; Magura at least 30
at 30 km/h; Magura says come to a full stop and TRP says do not. SRAM calls bed-in "critical for
new brake systems, pads, and rotors" at about 10 minutes. `Coach judgment:` follow the brake
maker's own sequence, and do it days before a race, not race morning, so noise or fade shows up
with time to fix.

## 2. Pads, contamination, rub (UNREVIEWED)

**Pad choice (manufacturer, pad-seller interest).** SRAM says sintered pads excel in all conditions,
especially wet and muddy, but are louder and need more bed-in; organic pads are not the best choice
for wet or muddy conditions and wear faster when wet, but are quieter and bite earlier. Park Tool's
chart rates organic best for initial bite and rotor life but shortest pad life, and metallic longest.
**This pad recommendation rests on commercial sources only.** `Coach judgment:` for a muddy CX race
or a 24 h with rain, fit new sintered or metallic pads, check thickness, carry a spare set. Park
Tool: pads should measure at least 1 mm.

**Contamination (tool maker).** Oil, grease and fluids ruin pads, and contamination is not always
visible; signs are squeal, extra lever force and a dark smearing surface. Find the source, commonly
aerosol lube overspray or a hydraulic leak. Oil-soaked pads were not restorable in Park Tool's case
study. `Coach judgment:` no degreaser, chain lube or silicone spray near rotors; replace
contaminated pads.

**Rotor rub (tool maker).** Rub is the main symptom of a misaligned caliper; other causes are a poorly
seated wheel, bent rotor, loose rotor mounting, hub bearing play and sticky pistons. Park Tool's
alignment: loosen both caliper bolts, squeeze the lever to center, tighten, recheck, then torque
bolts to about 6-8 Nm. `Coach judgment:` check wheel seating first on every wheel swap, which
matters for CX pit wheels.

## 3. Wheels and spokes (UNREVIEWED)

Tool maker (Park Tool): spokes have a range of acceptable tightness, uneven tension will not stay
true, and tension is best measured with a spoke tension meter. As a guideline stop truing at 0.5 mm
or less lateral and 1 mm or less radial, though the stopping point is highly subjective. Their
method for wind-up is to ride the wheel and expect some pinging and popping; bladed spokes can twist
with the nipple, so hold them with a spoke holder. `Coach judgment:` have a shop check tension
evenness with a meter before a season or big event; do not true a wheel the night before unless it
is visibly out. Use the rim maker's tension number; none was verified here.

## 4. Tubeless sealant (UNREVIEWED)

Practitioner (Kopecky (2019), Slowtwitch): manufacturers suggest about one year of shelf life;
replenishment is typically every 2-12 months; if in doubt check at least every six months; dry
climates need more frequent checks; remove tires annually to clear dried sealant. An independent
tester notes a new tubeless setup took three days to seal fully (Bierman (2014)).
`Coach judgment:` for races, check and top up sealant 1-2 weeks out, not the night before; ride a
new tire a few days before racing; carry plugs and a tube for a cut that will not seal.

## 5. Drivetrain lube (UNREVIEWED)

**[ADAPTED: general-endurance]** In a magazine lab test (TOUR (2025); published method), 20 degreased
chains ran an hour at 370 W; a good wax saved 5.5 W versus factory lube (about 1.5%), and losses
across products ran from about 11.5 to 16.7 W. **The main test did not cover wet or dirty chains, so
there is no mud data.** The same article says waxed chains protect against corrosion less well than
oil, and that 200 km per application is more realistic than makers' claimed 1,000 km. Confidence:
medium for dry efficiency, low for mud. Test: the gain is small and condition-dependent; compare
chain noise and re-lube frequency across a few rides rather than chasing watts.

`Coach judgment:` in wet or muddy CX and long wet events choose a lube that survives water and
re-lube after washing; use dry lube or wax in dry conditions if the athlete maintains it.

## 6. Torque (UNREVIEWED)

Park Tool gives 6-8 Nm for caliper bolts. `Coach judgment:` use the number printed on the part; use a
torque wrench on carbon bars, posts and stems; recheck stem, bar, brake, axle, crank, cleat and rotor
bolts before big events.

## 7. CX pit and kit specifics (UNREVIEWED)

Practitioner thin evidence: Cyclocross Magazine says staying on the bike for a rear-wheel swap is
faster, and a waiting bike is faster than a wheel swap. **Everything else here is coach
judgment**, as no source covered pit-bike matching. Both bikes get the same cleats, saddle height,
brake lever feel and gearing; the pit wheel is set at the planned pressure; the pit helper practises
the swap; wheels are fully seated so discs do not rub. In mud, clean and re-lube the chain between
laps and check pad thickness after a wet race.

## 8. Pre-race checklist and long events (`Coach judgment:`, partly from a Singletracks 24 h guide)

- **1-2 weeks out:** new pads if under 1 mm, sealant check, chain and cassette wear check, wheels
  true with even tension, full bolt check, brakes bedded.
- **Night before:** tire pressure only, wash and lube, a quick spin check.
- **Do not change the night before:** contact points, cleat position, saddle height, tire model,
  unbedded pads or rotors, chain or cassette, sealant, gearing.
- **6-24 h events:** fresh pads and chain at the start; spare hanger and quick link; plug kit and a
  tube; lights tested with spare batteries; a re-lube plan at pit stops; a pad check at midpoint in
  mud; re-torque bolts at a pit. The guide also lists spare cables, nuts and bolts, chainring bolts,
  and an extra set of tires or wheels for changing weather.

## 9. Contact points and fit (out of scope)

This library does not provide a bike fit. A systematic review of knee pain in cyclists found limited
evidence, all retrospective (Bini and Bini (2018)). `Coach judgment:` change one contact point at a
time in small steps, not before a key event; treat new pain as a stop-and-assess signal; refer to a
qualified fitter.
